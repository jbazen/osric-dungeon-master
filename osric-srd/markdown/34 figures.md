# OSRIC — Figures and Maps (non-verbatim)

*OSRIC — editorial figure descriptions, not verbatim transcription. Companion to the 33 verbatim files; see `03 file manifest.md`'s "Separate, non-verbatim" entry for why this file exists outside the verbatim guarantee.*

*Built directly from the PDF's own page images (rasterised fresh this batch, 300 dpi), not from any of the 33 transcribed markdown files — this file does not depend on, and is not subject to, the batch 18 provisional-audit flag carried in the headers of the master index and `osric.md`. The five-figure inventory that pass produced was closed by a full raster-and-vector pass across all 412 pages in the project's early batches, and reconfirmed with nothing new found by every subsequent full-method transcriber's own page rasterisation, batch after batch, through batch 17. Batch 18 did not re-run that survey; it rebuilt these five descriptions and their image crops from the source pages, and left the one still-open candidate (p.177) exactly as open as batch 6 left it. **Owner ruling, taken after batch 18: the p.177 illustration is included.** It is the sixth entry below, added by a later remedy pass which established its page geometry and description fresh from PDF page 189; its PNG crop has not been exported yet — see Assets.*

*Descriptions only — nothing here is a transcription of printed text, except where a figure carries no other typeset content and a label is quoted for identification. Each verbatim file that touches one of these figures carries its own pointer comment (e.g. `<!-- FIGURE p.164: see 34 figures.md -->`); this file is what those pointers resolve to.*

## Example Dungeon — cave-mouth illustration

**Book p.153 (PDF 165).** Referenced by a `NEW`... no — by a plain FIGURE pointer in `14b example dungeon and sample play.md`, at the head of the "AN EXAMPLE DUNGEON" section.

Half-tone pen-and-ink illustration, dense cross-hatching, positioned at the top of the left column above the "EXAMPLE DUNGEON KEY" heading. Depicts a rocky cave mouth framed by jagged rock spires. An armoured, helmeted figure stands on a rock outcropping at the centre of the scene, gesturing toward the cave entrance. To the right, a hooded figure holds a lit torch beside the cave mouth itself, over which a sign reads "WELCO—" (cropped by the illustration's own frame edge; almost certainly "WELCOME," matching the scene's tone, but the full word is not visible in the printed art). A skull and scattered bones lie at the lower left. Purely decorative header art for the worked example — the functional map is the following page's figure, below.

## Example Dungeon — map

**Book p.154 (PDF 166).** The map the p.153 dungeon key (in `14b example dungeon and sample play.md`) is unusable without, per the manifest's own note. Also referenced by a FIGURE pointer in the same file.

A near-full-page, hand-drawn dungeon map on a graph-paper background, printed below two columns of worked GM/player dialogue at the top of the page. A stream (a wavy, hatched watercourse) winds in from the west edge of the map, matching the key's own description of "a small stream flowing into it." A circular chamber near the entrance carries a rubble/rock-pile feature at its centre. The bulk of the map is a grid of interconnected rooms of mixed shapes — rectangular, octagonal, hexagonal — linked by corridors, door tick-marks at most room junctions, and a small spiral-stair icon in one chamber. A "$" mark appears at one point on the map (likely a treasure/goods-storage location), and an "X" mark appears at two others. The map's outer border is inked as solid cavern rock, with a dotted/stippled passage running off the bottom-right edge — an unmapped continuation beyond the drawn area.

**No legend, scale, or room numbers are printed on the map itself.** The "Map Key" list in `14b example dungeon and sample play.md` (Room 1 "Guard Cave" through Room 5 "Goods Storage," plus the un-numbered Arsenal and Dog Kennel entries) must be matched to the drawing by the key's own geographic description (stream entry in the northwest, etc.), not by any printed numeral on the map. Worth flagging for whoever runs this at the table — the map and the key do not cross-reference each other by number, only by the prose's own sense of direction.

Crop: `34 figures assets/example-dungeon-map-p154.png`

## Room-shape panels, I–VI

**Book p.164 (PDF 176).** Referenced by a FIGURE pointer in `15 dungeons and generation.md`, consumed directly by that file's Table 3 ("Special Rooms or Chambers") and Table 5 ("Number of Exits"), both of which are printed immediately below the figure on the same page.

A full-width figure at the top of the page: six labelled panels, roman numerals I–VI, arranged three across and two down, each a hand-drawn dungeon-chamber outline on the same graph-paper style background with hatched (shaded) walls indicating rock:

- **I** — a four-way, cross/plus-shaped chamber with a short stub passage exiting each of the four points.
- **II** — an irregular zigzag chamber, roughly H- or Z-shaped, with a passage exiting along the bottom.
- **III** — an irregular chamber in the same style as II, with a passage exiting the top (through a short hatched stair-like notch) and another exiting one side.
- **IV** — an oval/lens-shaped chamber, a passage exiting the top and a stairway feature at the bottom.
- **V** — an octagonal chamber with a circular, sunburst-hatched feature at its centre (a pool, well, or shaft — matching Table 3's own footnote that a "Circular" room roll can add a pool, well, or shaft) and passages exiting two opposite sides.
- **VI** — a cross-shaped chamber containing two small nested square inner features (readable as pillars or a dais, not labelled) and a hatched ramp or stair at the bottom.

Table 3 itself generates a broader set of named shapes (Cave, Circular, Hexagonal, Octagonal, Oval, Special, Trapezoidal, Triangular) than the six sketched panels cover one-for-one, and its own "Special" result already instructs the GM to "freehand draw an unusual shape" rather than pointing at a fixed panel — so the six panels read as illustrative reference sketches for the generation procedure, not as a lookup table indexed by die roll.

Crop: `34 figures assets/room-shape-panels-p164.png`

## Wilderness landscape — waterfall and bear

**Book p.177 (PDF 189).** In `16a urban and wilderness encounters.md`'s range, immediately after that file's `<!-- p.177 -->` marker. **No FIGURE pointer currently exists in file 16a** — every other inventoried figure except the character sheet has one, so a pointer comment (`<!-- FIGURE p.177: see 34 figures.md -->`) is still owed there; adding it was outside the scope of the pass that added this entry. Open as a sixth-figure candidate since batch 6 and left out by every batch through batch 18 as purely decorative, on the same reasoning as the p.245 unicorn; added here on the owner's ruling.

A full-width landscape illustration at the top of the page: a monochrome tonal engraving in the nineteenth-century scenic-print manner, not line art. It occupies (48.00, 40.99)–(383.76, 253.69) pt on the 419.76 × 595.44 pt page — 335.76 × 212.70 pt, aspect 1.58 — and bridges both text columns (column 1 runs x 48.0–211.4, column 2 x 221.9–385.3), so it spans the full text measure and the gutter between them. It is a **raster** image, not vector art: a single DCTDecode (JPEG) DeviceGray object, 1399 × 887 px, placed at exactly 300 dpi; the page carries no vector drawing objects at all, unlike the p.i device and the character-sheet form. Nothing is printed above it but the page's own running head and folio (`177` / `DUNGEONS, TOWNS AND WILDERNESSES`, y 23.26–31.22); body text resumes 7.6 pt below its lower edge, at y 261.26, and runs from there to the foot of the page as one unbroken two-column band — there is no crop seam and no text wraps around the figure.

The scene: a stand of tall conifers filling the left third, with snow-capped mountain peaks and a deep hanging valley behind them; a waterfall spilling in stages over pale rock ledges at centre-right, feeding a still lake or river pool that occupies the whole foreground right; broadleaf trees and a sheer cliff face along the right edge. A single bear walks left-to-right along the near shore in the lower-left foreground, past a fallen log and boulders. **The illustration carries no caption, no label, no lettering of any kind inside its frame, no artist signature and no credit line** — confirmed on the page image at 1200 dpi along the bottom edge, where a credit would sit. It cross-references nothing: the surrounding text (the tail of the Lost World encounter notes and the "Explanation of NPC Types" list) neither mentions it nor depends on it. Decorative chapter art, inventoried here for completeness rather than for use at the table.

Crop: `34 figures assets/wilderness-landscape-p177.png`

## Player Character Reference Sheet

**Book pp.390–391 (PDF 402–403), Appendix B.** Not transcribed as body text — `03 file manifest.md`'s "Not transcribed" table covers it explicitly: *"A vector form. Extraction yields floating field labels only; the structure is line art. No worker is to reconstruct it — that would be invention, not transcription."* What follows is a description of the printed structure, not a reconstruction of it as a fillable form, and the field ordering below follows only what is directly visible on the page.

A two-page blank character-record form, printed in a landscape reading direction relative to the book's own portrait page (the sheet reads across the wider dimension; a user filling it in turns the sheet, not the surrounding book pages), split across p.390 (recto) and p.391 (verso). No values are pre-filled on either page.

**Page 390** carries, top to bottom:

- A header block: the OSRIC wordmark, "OLD SCHOOL REFERENCE AND INDEX COMPILATION," and the sheet's own title, "PLAYER CHARACTER REFERENCE SHEET."
- **"Personal Attributes:"** — a bordered field grid with labelled boxes for Name, Class(es*), Alignment, Race, XP†, HP, AC, Lvl‡, Age, Height, Weight, and Sex, plus two footnote rows keyed to the asterisk/dagger/double-dagger marks: "*Multiple Classes:", "†Multiple Classes:", "‡Multiple Classes:" — fields for a character who is multi-classed or dual-classed to record the additional class(es)' own XP and level separately.
- **"Abilities:"** — a table with one row per ability score (Str, Dex, Con, Int, Wis, Cha) and, per row, a set of labelled derived-stat field boxes specific to that ability rather than a single fixed column header repeated across all six rows — e.g. the Str row's own fields include To Hit, Damage, and a Major Test box; the Dex row's include Missile To Hit, Surprise, AC, and a Minor Test box; and so on down through Cha's Loyalty and Reaction fields. The exact field-to-row layout is set by the printed grid lines themselves and is not restated field-by-field here to avoid inventing an ordering the page states only visually — see the crop.
- **"Save vs …:"** — five labelled field boxes in a row: Death/Paralysis/Poison, Petrifaction/Polymorph, Breath Weapons, Aimed Magic Items, Spells.
- **"Weapons & Armour:"** — a "Roll required to hit Armour Class" grid running AC +10 down to AC −10 across its columns (the same span as the class To Hit tables in the book body and Appendix A), with a "Weapons" field block above it (Weapons, Damage vs S-M, Damage vs L, Rate of Fire, Range) and an "Armour/Protection" / "AC" field block below, plus a separate "Encumbrance" field column.

**Page 391** carries:

- **"Equipment:"** — a wide, multi-row blank field block.
- **"Wealth:"** — a field block with several ruled columns of differing widths (evidently intended to be split by coin type, matching the book's own coinage system) but with no column headers printed.
- **"Special Abilities (race):"** and **"Special Abilities (class):"** — each a multi-line blank field block.
- **"Notes:"** — a final multi-line blank field block.

Crops: `34 figures assets/character-reference-sheet-p390.png`, `34 figures assets/character-reference-sheet-p391.png`
